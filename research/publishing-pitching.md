# Research Digest: Publishing Routes, Pitching & Industry (Board Game Design)

Sources fetched directly (Sep 2026): Stonemaier Games blog + submission guidelines; League of Gamemakers (LOG); James Mathe (via Wayback); Joe Slack; Brandon the Game Dev; Cardboard Edison; UK Games Expo; Unpub; Gen Con press releases; Wikipedia (Gen Con/SPIEL attendance).

---

## Core frameworks & models

- **The Three Routes** (community consensus framing): (1) *License* to an established publisher for royalties — you design, they fund everything else; (2) *Self-publish*, usually via Kickstarter/Gamefound — you keep control and upside but run a business; (3) *Work-for-hire* — flat-fee design on a publisher's brief/IP, no royalties, no credit guarantee.
- **Stegmaier's 4-Step Pitch Process** (Jamey Stegmaier, 2022): Preparation (know the market, finish a unique game, playtest, polished prototype) → Selection (shortlist ~10 publishers whose line fits; pick ONE game) → Submission (follow their guidelines exactly; lead with hook + "X meets Y with a twist"; sell yourself) → Conclusion (wait patiently; prepare next-step materials; accept rejection with grace).
- **Publisher decision heuristics — Stonemaier's screening questions**: Does it fit our mission/brand vibe? Do we already publish something similar (mechanically or thematically)? Has another publisher made a close facsimile? Does it have evergreen big-hit potential (incl. value proposition: estimated price vs. needed price)? Are we excited to teach it repeatedly? Does it slot into the line (player count, weight, length, hook)?
- **Submission funnel** (Stonemaier): form → rulebook/gameplay video → requested prototype → full-team play → written feedback round (tests response to criticism) → contract → development.
- **Mathe's Courting Etiquette** (James Mathe, Minion Games, 2013): the canonical pitch-etiquette list — research publisher fit, 1-paragraph hook-first emails, list vitals (time/age/players/mechanisms/expected MSRP) + component totals, no NDAs, no unsolicited prototypes, parallel submissions OK if disclosed, prototype stage implies exclusivity.
- **Royalty-base taxonomy** (Tom Jolly, "Game Contract Pitfalls," LOG, 2014; Stegmaier 2022): royalties computed on one of four bases — % of MSRP, % of wholesale/gross revenue, % of net receipts, % of profit. Revenue-based strongly advised over profit-based (expenses can be "creatively" deducted).
- **Escalating royalty** (Joe Slack, 2023): rate steps up after milestones (e.g., 5% to 5,000 units, then 6-7%); in nearly all of Slack's contracts.
- **Work-for-hire staged delivery** (Tom Jolly): milestones — concept → raw prototype → playtested prototype → revisions → final — each with dates, partial payments, late penalties, ideally a kill fee. Standard for designing on a publisher's IP (his example: FFG's Mansions of Madness module).
- **Landed-cost pricing rule** (James Mathe, 2013): MSRP = per-unit landed cost (print + freight + fees) × 5-6; i.e., landed cost ≤ ~20% of MSRP.

## Catalog / techniques

**Licensing deal anatomy** (Stegmaier contract post + Jolly):
- *Product definition*: all physical + digital versions, expansions, accessories, localizations in one definition.
- *Advance against royalties*: paid at signing, recouped from future royalties, non-refundable.
- *Payment cadence*: quarterly or semiannual is standard, within 30 days of period end (Slack, Jolly); Stonemaier pays monthly.
- *Responsibilities*: designer completes design + revisions; publisher funds/executes everything else (development, blind playtesting, art, manufacturing, marketing, distribution) and holds final say with "reasonable and respectful consideration" of designer input.
- *Term & termination*: perpetual until terminated (Stonemaier) or fixed years (Jolly). Triggers to demand: failure to publish within ~2 years of development start; failure to pay within 30 days of written demand; printing fewer than a minimum (e.g., 1,000 units) over 3 years; material breach.
- *Sell-off*: ~1 year to sell remaining stock after termination.
- *Sublicensing*: claim 20-40% of sublicense fees, or royalty-per-copy regardless of licensee; geographic splits (e.g., North America only) preserve other-region rights (Jolly).
- *Digital rights*: retain if possible; license per platform, non-exclusive (Jolly sold Wiz-War computer rights 2-3 times).
- *Designer name on box*: put it in writing (Stegmaier). Hobby norm since mid-2010s: designers marketed as brands (Wingspan sold as "by Elizabeth Hargrave"; Knizia/Rosenberg as selling points).

**Self-publishing via crowdfunding** (Mathe 2013, Stegmaier KS Lessons, Brandon the Game Dev):
- Distribution economics: distributors pay ~40% of MSRP (and want free shipping); direct-to-retailer ~50%; via fulfillment house ~34%.
- First print runs: 1,500-2,500 copies (Mathe).
- Setup costs (2013, directionally valid): punchboard die ~$300 each; mini mold $3,000-5,000; custom dice rarely worth it under 2,000 units; white-box sample $100-300; 50% down payment to start printing.
- Kickstarter mechanics: fees 5% + ~3-5% processing; most popular pledge tier $20-30; ~30-day campaigns; signed copies +$10-20; add-content rewards +$25-50; video <3 min; post in BGG's Kickstarter announcement thread; secure third-party reviews pre-launch.
- Component specs: cards 275-300 gsm; boards 1-2 mm linen finish; card sizes Euro 59×91/44×67 mm, US 63×88/57×87 mm; 300 dpi CMYK; 3 mm bleed; UPC + age + time + choking hazard on back, NO PRICE on box.
- Pipeline timeline (Mathe): ~1 year total; printing 60-90 days; ocean freight 21-30 days.

**Work-for-hire**: flat/milestone payment, no royalties; contract defines stages, per-stage payments, kill fee (rare; Jolly got one uncontracted from WotC), credit terms. Outright IP sale alternative: expect "at least double" a typical advance (Jolly). Modern exemplars: design studios Prospero Hall (Disney Villainous, Horrified, Jaws for Ravensburger) and Forrest-Pruzan Creative work on publisher briefs; mass-market publishers (Hasbro, Spin Master, Funko) also employ salaried in-house designers (Slack).

**Sell sheet anatomy** (Laurie/LOG): title + key art, hook, vitals (player count, age, time), component totals (not details), brief mechanism description, special features ("make every sentence a selling point" — Andrew Federspiel), theme/marketability cues, designer name + contact. Emailed PDFs under 2 MB. Jay Cormier: "a clean sell sheet tells the publisher you are professional." Stonemaier's form requests sell sheet, visual overview, and/or short video of a few turns + favorite mechanisms.

**Pitch channels**:
- Publisher submission forms (follow exactly; circumventing = red flag — Stegmaier).
- Convention speed dating: Gen Con speed dating historically hosted by James Mathe/Minion Games (per LOG 2014); UK Games Expo official Publisher-Designer Speed Dating — 5-minute rotations; requires sell sheet + rules sheet + optional pitch video; 24-36 designs selected from 100+ applicants.
- Playtesting circuit where publishers scout: Unpub (The Unpublished Games Network, founded 2010 by John Moller, MD/PA/DE nucleus, free public playtests), Protospiel (regional + online), Metatopia (NJ, design-focused), Stonemaier's annual Design Day (no pitches; they look at whatever designers bring).
- Video pitches: pre-recorded playthrough video is a standard next-step request before a prototype is asked for; Tabletopia/TTS versions increasingly accepted.

**Major conventions**: Gen Con (Indianapolis, Aug) — largest in North America: 71,000 attendees, 540+ exhibiting companies (2024); 2018 press release: 60,000+ unique attendees, 520+ game companies, 600+ new games, 17,000 ticketed events. Essen SPIEL (Oct, since 1983) — world's biggest: 220,000 attendees, 948 exhibitors from 50 nations, 1,700+ new releases (2025); primary European publisher-meeting venue. Origins (Columbus, GAMA, Origins Awards). UK Games Expo (Birmingham) — speed dating + Playtest Zone. Dice Tower Con (Orlando). Designer-centric: Unpub, Protospiel, Metatopia.

**BGG ecosystem**: Game Design forums + Work-in-Progress (WIP) threads for feedback; community-run design contests (print-and-play, two-player, etc.) with GeekGold prize pools; GeekGold (GG) is BGG's internal currency — earned via contributions (reviews, images, files, tips), spent on avatars/badges and contest bounties; the BGG Kickstarter announcement thread is standard launch practice. Ranking reality: "Geek Rating" is a Bayesian average padding each game with dummy votes at 5.5 to damp small-sample games, distinct from raw "Average Rating" [dummy count undisclosed, changed over time — any precise figure is approximate]; rank/hotness drive retail & publisher attention but are hobbyist-skewed.

**Reviewer/preview/media ecosystem**: The Dice Tower (Tom Vasel; reviews unpaid, *previews* are paid advertising and labeled as such — "preview ≠ review" is their disclosure mechanism), Shut Up & Sit Down (do not sell coverage), Rahdo (runthroughs; historically charged, disclosed; Patreon-supported), Watch It Played (teaching videos), Man vs Meeple, No Pun Included, Actualol. Paid previews are broadly accepted *if labeled*; the controversy (BGG threads ~2015-2017) centers on undisclosed payment and pay-to-preview barriers for small publishers. Norm: reviewers need comp copies and lead time; previews are marketing buys, reviews are earned.

**Agents**: effectively nonexistent in the hobby market — designers pitch publishers directly; no major hobby publisher routes submissions through agents. They exist in the mass-market/toy-invention world (inventor agents repping portfolios to Hasbro/Mattel-type companies for a cut of royalties) [fee percentages not standardized — contested].

**Design contests**: Cardboard Edison Award — annual, for unpublished designs (new or published designers); rulebook + pitch video submission; physical prototype mailed if finalist (international OK; English rulebook or language-neutral); entry fee per design; complete, thoroughly playtested games score better; AI art discouraged; publisher judges (frequent route to licensing; licensing talks barred during judging). Others: Hippodice (German), Premio Archimede (Italian), BGG community contests, publisher open calls (e.g., Panda GM's $2,000-20,000 manufacturing-credit contest).

## Numbers, heuristics & rules of thumb

- **Royalty rate**: most contracts 5-8% of net sales (Jolly; Slack); extremes 2%-10% (Jolly); Stonemaier pays 7-10% of *gross sales revenue* to the "design unit" (split among co-designers); Mathe (2013): 3-5% of MSRP ≈ 5-6% of wholesale ≈ 20-25% of net profit. Same economics, different bases — always convert before comparing.
- **Wholesale ≈ 40% of MSRP** (Slack; Mathe). So 5% of wholesale ≈ 2% of MSRP ≈ $1/unit on a $50 game (Slack's example: $50 retail → $20 wholesale → $1/unit → $1,000 on 1,000 units/quarter).
- **Advances**: rare for new designers (Slack: usually only larger publishers; small KS-first publishers can't). Jolly: a few hundred to $5,000; a company offering no advance "isn't serious" [contested — many legit small publishers pay none]. Stonemaier (2021+): flat $10,000 — a generous outlier.
- **Unit economics** (Stegmaier): $50 MSRP → $20 distributor price; $10 manufacturing; $2 freight; $2 designer royalty (10%); $6 publisher gross covers art, marketing, staff, reprint. Example first print run: 18,000 to distributors at $20 + 2,000 direct at $50 → designer earns $32,200 at 10%.
- **MSRP tiers** (Mathe 2013): single-deck card game $9-19; light family/party $20-39; standard 12"×12" game $50-60; big-box up to $99 (modern equivalents ~15-30% higher).
- **Print runs**: indie first run 1,500-2,500 (Mathe); Stonemaier minimum 20,000+ per new title. Contract minimum-print clause: 1,000 units/3 years (blocks POD "publication" loopholes — Jolly).
- **Acceptance odds**: Stonemaier published 5 of ~400 lifetime submissions (~1.25%) as of 2016; ~3,000 submissions over 9 years of their form; UKGE speed dating picks 24-36 of 100+ applicants. Playtesting bar: "playtested at least 10 times" (Stonemaier form).
- **Timelines**: design alone 8-18 months per game (Stegmaier); concept→fulfillment case: 10 months for a simple card game (Tricorn Games via Brandon the Game Dev); contract-to-market obligation commonly ~2 years (Mathe; Stegmaier's 2-year release clause); the widely cited "18-24 months from signed contract to shelf" is consistent with the above.
- **Art costs (2013, dated)**: box cover $100-500; game board $100-300; card art $25-100/card; layout/manual $500-2,000 (Mathe).
- **Earnings reality**: "very few game designers can make a living doing this full-time" (Slack); royalties rarely equal a salary unless the game is a hit or the designer has multiple steady sellers (Stegmaier).

## Best-practice checklists

**Before pitching**:
1. Know the market (reviews, BGG, play widely); confirm your game doesn't already exist.
2. Finish the design — "ideas are largely worthless" (Stonemaier).
3. Playtest ≥10 times + blind playtest with strangers; rulebook a stranger can learn from; accurate parts list.
4. Build an attractive thematic prototype (clip art OK — do NOT commission art).
5. Prepare: sell sheet (1 page, <2 MB), 1-paragraph hook email, rules PDF, 1 small photo, short gameplay video.
6. Shortlist ~10 publishers from your own shelf; verify guidelines and line fit (player count, weight, length, theme no-lists).
7. Pitch ONE game with a hook ("Ark Nova meets The Crew… with time travel").

**Pitch email anatomy** (Mathe): 1-paragraph hook-first description → vitals (time, age, players, mechanisms, expected MSRP) → component totals → target audience → 1 small image → rules PDF link → offer of video/sell sheet. No development story, no theme essay, no NDA request, no multi-game dump.

**Follow-up cadence**: max once a month, quarterly better; acceptable to request final answer or prototype return after ~1 year (may trigger return instead); disclose parallel submissions; prototype stage = de facto exclusivity.

**Contract review** (Jolly + Stegmaier): royalty on revenue not profit; advance non-refundable; termination date regardless of publication + minimum-print clause ≥1,000 copies (blocks POD loophole); ~2-year publish-or-revert; sell-off ≤1 year; sublicensing 20-40% or per-copy royalty; retain digital & foreign rights where possible; name-on-box clause; hire a contract lawyer (contracts run 1-20 pages; Stegmaier's is 3).

**Speed dating prep** (UKGE model): playable prototype (concept ≠ enough), sell sheet, rules sheet, optional pitch video, rehearsed 5-minute pitch, follow-up plan.

**Self-publishing readiness**: crowdbuild BEFORE launch (mailing list, BGG thread, social); third-party review secured; $20-30 pledge tier; <3-min video; 30-day campaign; stretch goals = component upgrades, never new rules; white-box sample before full run; 5-6× landed-cost MSRP check.

## Common pitfalls & failure modes

- **The NDA request**: asking a publisher to sign an NDA or fretting about idea theft — instant newbie tell; publishers have plenty of their own ideas (Mathe). Don't bother patenting, either.
- **Unsolicited prototypes**: forms and guidelines exist as filters — circumventing them is a red flag (Stegmaier).
- **The bag o' games**: offering a menu of designs instead of championing one — "not an asset" (Stegmaier).
- **Unplayable submission prototypes**: Stonemaier's #1 observed mistake — missing reference cards, broken rules; solved by blind playtesting. One first impression.
- **Wrong-line submissions**: war game to a casual publisher; too-similar to their catalog; themes on their no-list (Stonemaier rejects historical/religious themes, campaign/legacy, low player ceilings).
- **Profit-based royalties**: enables "fancy accounting" that shrinks your cut (Stegmaier); always revenue-based.
- **Open-ended term / POD loophole**: "expires 2 years after first production" = rights held forever if never produced; a 100-copy POD run counts as "published" (Jolly's lived examples).
- **Rights hostage**: publisher sits on the game for years without publishing (happened to Jolly ~5 times) — mitigate with advance + firm termination.
- **Loss-leader loophole**: your game given away free to market something else, generating no royalty (Jolly's Wiz-War online case).
- **Sublicensing dilution**: friendly companies swap rights cheaply, shrinking your cut; or the publisher acquires purely to flip (Jolly).
- **Hiring your own artist**: publishers discard commissioned art; wasted money (Mathe).
- **Pitching too early**: the "Almost Finished Board Game" (Brandon Rollins) — publishers judge what they touch; WIP feel kills interest.
- **Crowdfunding without a crowd**: launching KS before building an audience; backers are the gatekeeper.
- **Day-job math**: royalties arrive quarterly, years after signing, only if the game ships and sells — not a salary.
- **Theme rigidity**: appearing unwilling to change theme/mechanisms — publishers hold the financial risk, so they hold final say (Mathe; Stegmaier).
