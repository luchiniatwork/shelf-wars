# Components, Manufacturing & Production — Research Digest

## Core frameworks & models

- **The Basic Five (Panda Game Manufacturing, Graphic Design Guidebook v4, 2022)** — Panda's five file-prep requirements for all print files: (1) submit as PDFs, (2) CMYK color only (never RGB), (3) resolution ≥300 ppi, (4) ≥3mm bleed AND ≥3mm margin (safe zone) on every side, (5) black text in pure black (C0 M0 Y0 K100) set to overprint (never rich black — multi-plate misregistration blurs thin text).
- **Panda's 6-step production pipeline with 4 QC gates** — (1) Quote → (2) Design Verification (prepress check; common failures: low-res images, RGB images, spot colors, dielines embedded in art layers) → (3) Pre-Production Copy (PPC: near-final prototype of printed components air-shipped free, plus samples of non-printed parts, reviewed on a video call) → (4) Mass Production + Mass Production Copy (MPC: first full final copy incl. shrinkwrap — last checkpoint) → (5) Assembly (final QC; Panda promises 99% defect-free and includes spare components free) → (6) Shipping (ocean/air/land).
- **The landed-cost pricing model (Jamey Stegmaier, "A Price Formula for Your Product," 2022)** — MSRP starts at manufacturing cost × 5, then adjusts for freight, royalties, discounts. Landed cost = manufacturing (benchmarked at 5,000-unit cost) + freight to warehouse. Worked example: Tuscany expansion costs $6.50 to make → 5× = $32.50; freight adds $3/unit → landed $9.50 → MSRP bumped to $35. Sold to distributors at 60% discount ($14) = $5.50/unit profit — insufficient alone, hence webstore/direct margins matter.
- **Mathe's multiplier rule (James Mathe, "10,000 Feet to Publishing a Board Game," 2013)** — MSRP = (total printing + shipping + fees ÷ number of games) × 5 or 6. This is the origin of the community "5x rule." Note the two versions differ: Mathe multiplies *landed* cost; Stegmaier multiplies *manufacturing* cost and adjusts.
- **Distribution chain economics (Stegmaier, "The Math of Tariffs," 2025)** — Baseline chain: $10 production → distributor pays publisher $20 → retailer pays distributor $25 → consumer pays $50 (MSRP). Direct-sale baseline: $10 production vs $50 consumer price, minus ~$20 fulfillment subsidy. Retailers need the 2× markup over their cost for cash-flow, overhead, and discount headroom.
- **MOQ/print-method tiers** — Digital/POD for 1–500; sheet-fed offset for ~250–10,000 units (PrintNinja: "best used for short or mid-range runs"); web offset for 10,000+. Chinese consolidated manufacturers (Panda et al.) typically require 1,000–1,500+.
- **Children's product regulatory threshold** — US CPSIA defines "children's product" as intended primarily for ≤12 years → triggers third-party testing, tracking labels, certificates. Panda's guidance: for games marked 13 and under, customs/distributors may demand EN-71/UKCA/ASTM F963 test results. Hence the industry-standard "14+" age label on hobby games.

## Catalog / techniques

**Cards.** Panda standard sizes (cards per press sheet): Mini 44×67mm (84/sheet), Mini Square 51×51 (110), Square 70×70 (56), Euro 59×91 (45), Blackjack/poker 63×88 (54), Bridge 57×87 (60). Mathe's shorthand: EURO = 59×91 & 44×67; USA = 63×88 & 57×87. PrintNinja: Poker 2.5×3.5″ (63.5×88.9mm), Tarot 2.75×4.75″ (≈70×120mm), Square 2.5×2.5″, Mini 1.65×2.5″, customs 1.5–6.9″. "Mini American" 41×63mm (FFG-standard) is common community usage [secondary source]. Standard corner radius 2.5mm (Panda). Stocks: cards typically 275–300gsm multi-ply (Mathe). PrintNinja menu: Blue Core 280gsm (economy), Coreless Standard 300, Coreless Plus 350, Black Core 310, Black Core Plus 330 (max opacity), Foil 300, White PVC 450 (waterproof). Chinese-printer jargon: ivory core and grey core (grey = cheap; specify e.g. "ivory core 350gsm" explicitly or you get the cheap default — Liminal via Stonemaier). Colored cores prevent see-through at edges. Deck submission: one multi-page PDF, fronts in order, common back as last page; multiple backs → separate fronts/backs PDFs.

**Finishes.** Linen emboss (crosshatch texture; Stegmaier: use on cards, mats, boxes, boards — slides less, feels premium; avoid only with tiny art details or when using reflective foil). Matte vs gloss: Stegmaier's rule — never glossy boards/cards/mats (overhead-light glare; mistake made on original Tuscany). Coatings/fx per Panda: varnish/lamination basics; spot UV (shiny raised coating), metallic ink, foil stamping, emboss/deboss, scratch-off (legacy games) — each supplied as separate file or layer in single ink color.

**Punchboard (Panda specs).** Board ≥15mm smaller in length & width than box top. Min token ~8×8mm; smallest edge of any shape ≥3mm. ≥6mm between any two dielines; each token needs its own 3mm bleed + 3mm margin; "whole board dieline" matching contract size; dielines as separate PDF or separate layer (never in art layers) — dielines drive the die-cut mold, and each unique die-cut = new tooling cost, so reuse one dieline across multiple boards. Label/number boards. Front edges come out slightly rounded; back art must be mirrored to front. LoGM real spec sheet: "60-pt chipboard" (≈1.5mm) for mats/tokens; Mathe: boards/tokens typically 1–2mm with linen finish; industry punchboard range 1.5–2.5mm [consistent across sources, thickness varies by SKU].

**Boards & boxes.** Game board: 18mm wrap-around bleed on front; double-sided backs 3mm smaller per side; Panda max board 700×1000mm. Mathe warns: player boards warp if printed on one side only — always print both. Two-piece (telescoping) box: 18mm bleed, 15mm wrap-around; Panda example template 235×156×50mm in 2mm greyboard. Panda rule of thumb: box ≥15mm larger in each dimension than largest component. Stegmaier's freight math: standard pallet holds 48 cartons; at 6 games/carton vs 4, freight = $3.47 vs $5.21 per game → on 30,000 copies a $52,200 difference. Bigger boxes also = more component damage (top-to-bottom movement). Standard box bottom markings: player count, time, age, UPC (pure black + 5mm clearance for lot number), publisher/distributor addresses (EU requires distributor address; UK requires distributor + importer), "Made in [country]" — customs requires country of origin on box.

**Wood.** Standard bits 8–20mm (Mathe); cubes 8mm, meeples 16mm standard/24mm large, discs 10/15mm (from LoGM's real quote spec). Machine-cut or laser-cut from sheet wood/acrylic; acrylic slightly pricier than wood. Decoration: paint, stains, ink washes, silkscreen, laser engraving (adds "deluxe" feel), heat transfer. Stegmaier: gamers prefer wood over plastic for meeples/custom resources; request rounded-corner dice; latex-free rubber bands.

**Plastic miniatures (Panda Component Guidebook).** Materials: hard plastics ABS, POM, HIPS, acrylic; soft PVC. Detail floor ≥0.4mm recommended (eye floor ~0.2mm; metal minis ≥0.2mm exaggerated). Thin sections (weapons, tails): ≥0.6mm diameter hard plastics, ≥1.5mm PVC. Avoid undercuts (force multi-part molds + factory assembly). Same sculpt in multiple colors = mold resets = cost; stock snap bases (20/25/50mm) avoid this. Pre-painted minis only economical >10,000 units. Submit STP/STEP (preferred) or STL; exaggerate facial features. Plan ≥7 months extra for custom plastics; ~2 weeks per cost estimate, optional 2–4 weeks optimization. Mathe (2013): figure mold $3,000–5,000 setup; token punch dies ~$300 each — share one die layout; custom dice rarely worth it <2,000 games (use silkscreen/heat transfer on stock dice; customize one face of resin dice to share molds).

**Trays/inserts.** Vacuum-formed plastic trays ~0.9mm thick, black default, optional clear lid; submit 2D/3D design + STP/STEP; engraved lines ≥3mm wide. Paper-pulp trays = sustainable alternative, higher tooling cost, longer lead time. Cardboard trays cheapest. Stegmaier: extra box space = damage risk; inserts help side-to-side, not top-to-bottom.

**Rulebooks.** Saddle-stitch binding is the board-game default; also perfect, spiral, casewrap. Page count must be multiple of 4; no two-page spreads in the submitted PDF; larger gutter margin if 32+ pages. Mathe: manuals 100–150gsm, 4/4 color CMYK, layout/design $500–2,000. Stegmaier: box-sized rulebooks are unwieldy — Libertalia used 165×240mm deliberately smaller than the box; no text illustrated into art (kills localization).

**Proofing.** Digital/prepress proof (design verification) → "white box" physical sample (Mathe: FedEx $100–300, worth it) → PPC (air-shipped free by Panda) → MPC (first full mass-production copy). Payment rhythm (Mathe): 50% down + setup fees (wire fee $20–40); final bill before printer ships.

**Compliance markings (Panda guidebook + Wikipedia).** EU: EN 71 (14 parts; key: 71-1 mechanical/physical, 71-2 flammability, 71-3 migration of elements) under the Toy Safety Directive; CE mark = manufacturer's self-declaration, criminal offence to affix non-compliantly; EU "authorised representative" address holds the Declaration of Conformity (Stonemaier fulfillment post). UK: UKCA (largely mirrors EN 71). US: ASTM F963 [Panda's guidebook prints "ASTM F693" once — typo; F963 correct] + CPSIA: lead ≤90ppm surface coatings, ≤100ppm substrate, certificates of conformance, tracking labels. Age-warning: 0–3 symbol min 10mm height, black/white/red, unalterable; warning triangle taller than the word "Warning"; approved texts: "Warning: Not suitable for children under 36 months" + "CHOKING HAZARD — Small parts. Not for children under 3 years." Testing realistically required for games marketed ≤13; Panda brokers third-party testing but the publisher retains responsibility. Mathe calls printer-sold testing for adult-hobby games "a racket" [contested — his opinion; legally mandatory for children's products].

**Freight/customs.** Sea freight priced by volume, not weight (Mathe: $50 game × 2,000 units ≈ $3,000–4,000 China/Europe→warehouse, 2013 dollars; customs fees a few hundred; 21–30 days ocean). Pallets: 18–21 cartons/pallet (Stonemaier 2024); 300–600 games/pallet (Mathe). US HTS for board games: 9504.90.60.00 "games played on boards of a special design" — base duty "Free" (0%), but 2025 US tariffs on Chinese goods added 54%+ (Stonemaier: their 2024 production spend ~$10M → potential +$5M). Ship DDP to the EU so the VAT-inclusive price is final; put UKCA next to CE. GS1 barcodes required by fulfillment/retail; hobby SKUs via HMA. Regional fulfillment picks (Stonemaier 2024): US — Miniature Market, Fulfillrite, Quartermaster Logistics; EU — Spiral Galaxy (full-service VAT); Canada — Asmodee Canada; AU/NZ/Asia — Aetherworks; forwarder ARC Global cited. Factory pre-packing cuts fulfillment cost. Latin America/Africa parcels unreliable → forwarders (Shipito, MyUS).

**Manufacturers & brokers.** Panda GM (Canadian-owned, Shenzhen factory; 400+ publishers, 57M+ games, 41 languages; MOQ 1,500). LongPack Games (Shanghai; similar 6-step flow; mini painting tiers). Whatz Games (China). PrintNinja (US-fronted China offset; MOQ 500 decks; instant online calculator). Hero Time Manufacturing (Hersh Glueck, China-based; QC/communication pitch). Ludo Fact (Germany; EU production). Cartamundi (Belgium; cards giant). Mathe's maintained Hitchhiker's Guide chart adds: Wingo, BangWee (MOQ 500, low price, "wood & dice sub-par"), GPI, AdMagic (US front, uses MeiJia in China), Quality Playing Cards, Delano, NSF Games, Annboli (MOQ 2000), Altenburger Spielladen (Germany, "excellent"), 360 Manufacturing (warned against: delays, errors). Mathe's picks: Panda, LongPack, Wingo, BangWee, GPI.

## Numbers, heuristics & rules of thumb

- MSRP = 5–6× per-unit cost (Mathe: ×5–6 on landed; Stegmaier: ×5 on manufacturing, adjust; some publishers use 6–8× on manufacturing or 5× on landed when freight is heavy). [Sources agree on magnitude, disagree on base.]
- Distributor discount ~60–65% off MSRP; direct retailer ~45–50% (Stegmaier 2022). Mathe 2013: sell to distributor at 40% of MSRP (i.e., 60% off), via fulfillment house 34% of MSRP, direct-to-retailer 50% of MSRP + free shipping.
- Designer royalty: 3–5% of MSRP, 5–6% of wholesale, or 20–25% of net profit (Mathe).
- MOQ: 1,500 common for Chinese board-game plants (LoGM, Panda); PrintNinja 500 decks; BangWee 500; offset sensible from ~250 (sheet-fed) but per-unit price drops hard at 1,000+. [MOQ is the most variable number — always get quotes.]
- Quote turnaround ~5–6 weeks (LoGM); ask ≥3 run sizes (1,500/2,000/3,000).
- First print run: don't be talked into more than 1,500–2,500 copies (Mathe). Reality check: of 80 new products through Mathe's fulfillment house in 2012, only 22 sold >500 retail units.
- Stocks/thickness: cards 275–300gsm (Mathe), blue core 280 / black core 310–330 (PrintNinja); boards/tokens 1–2mm (Mathe), 60pt chipboard ≈1.5mm (LoGM), box greyboard 2mm typical (Panda), industry punchboard 1.5–2.5mm.
- Bleed 3mm + margin 3mm; 18mm wrap bleed on box/board fronts; trim tolerance ±1mm (Panda).
- Tooling: ~$300 per punch die; $3,000–5,000 per plastic mini mold (Mathe 2013; "thousands per mold" is the consistent magnitude across sources); custom dice not worth it <2,000 units.
- Timeline: full process ~1 year (Mathe); production 60–90 days; ocean 21–30 days; Panda: plan ≥7 months total for custom plastics.
- KS price points (Mathe 2013 — inflate): 1-deck card game $9–19; light family/party $20–39; typical 3lb 12″×12″ game $50–60; big-box up to $99.
- Freight: ~$3,000–4,500 per 2,000-unit shipment (Mathe 2013); $3.47–5.21/game swing from carton packing (Stegmaier); pallet = 48 cartons / 300–600 games.
- Art costs (Mathe 2013): box cover $100–500; board $100–300; cards $25–100 each; sculptor $300–500; layout $500–2,000.
- Samples: white-box $100–300 shipping (Mathe); Panda PPC/MPC air-shipped free.
- Unit economics: PrintNinja 54-card deck + tuck box ≈ $5–7 @ 500 (blue core), black core $6–8; MPC price curve 63×88 deck $10.25 @ 1–5 → $3.05 @ 250–499 → $1.00 @ 5,000+.
- Tariffs (2025): +54% on Chinese imports at article time; pass-flat chain math ($10→$15 production → MSRP $50→$55) vs full multiplier (→ $75) (Stegmaier).

## Best-practice checklists

**RFQ spec sheet (per LoGM/Cornelius + Panda)** — list every component in metric: exact dims, stock/gsm, finish, colors (Pantone C where critical), quantities per game; include diagrams/photos for unusual parts (they used SketchUp renders for a grooved 3-part track); request quotes at 3 quantities from ~10 printers; expect clarification questions within 5–6 weeks; never let the printer assume (specify "ivory core 350gsm not grey core", "2.0mm box", exact Pantone).
**File prep (Panda Basic Five + extras)** — PDFs; CMYK; ≥300ppi; 3mm bleed/margin; pure-black overprint text; dielines as separate file/layer; special-fx layers single-ink; "border black" C40 M0 Y0 K100 for card borders; no text in rasterized art; keep templates from manufacturer generator; cards as multi-page PDFs; rulebook pages multiple of 4.
**Component design for freight** — box ≤ needed volume; punchboard ≤ box −15mm; reuse dielines; consider pre-packing at factory; target carton/pallet efficiency (48 cartons/pallet).
**Vendor vetting (Liminal via Stonemaier)** — beware Yes-Men ("we can do anything") and Cool Chads (apathetic); video-call before committing; watch how they handle language misunderstandings; be hyper-specific; consolidate changes (weekly tweak-drips get you dropped); ask manufacturer to summarize decisions by email after calls.
**Production QA** — get white-box sample; play the PPC; verify MPC incl. shrinkwrap; require spare components; print on both sides of player boards; symmetrical card backs (180° rotation happens); air holes in bags; easy-peel shrink; label/number punchboards.
**Compliance** — decide target markets early; 14+ label to avoid children's-product regime unless kid-focused; box bottom: players/time/age/UPC/addresses/country of origin; 0–3 warning graphic unaltered; CE + UKCA; EU authorized-rep address; third-party test if ≤13.

## Common pitfalls & failure modes

- **RGB/low-res/dieline-in-art rejection at design verification** (Panda's most common prepress failures).
- **"I expected X but got Y"** — underspecification; factories default to cheapest option (grey-core cards, thinner board) when not told otherwise.
- **One-sided player boards warp** (Mathe).
- **Glossy boards = glare** (Stonemaier's Tuscany mistake).
- **Asymmetric card backs reveal 180° sheet rotation** across copies.
- **Undercuts in minis** → multi-piece molds, factory assembly, blown budgets; multi-color sculpts → mold-reset fees.
- **Oversized boxes** → freight bleed + component damage; 50% of gamers discard expansion boxes (Stonemaier poll).
- **SKU/add-on sprawl** → fulfillment errors + per-item fees.
- **Weekly change-drips** → manufacturer disengagement (Liminal).
- **QC horror stories** (Mathe): paper-stock-for-card-stock swaps to lower bids, misprints, QC staff writing on components — mitigated by factory reps on site (Panda model) and third-party inspection.
- **Safety-testing upsell for adult hobby games** [contested: Mathe calls it a racket; legally mandatory for ≤12 products].
- **Run-size errors**: overprinting burns cash; underprinting starves evergreen reprints (Wingspan 25th printing, Scythe 28th).
- **Tariff/geopolitical exposure** (2025): China-centric manufacturing faced 54–145% US tariffs; mitigations discussed include regional manufacturing (Earthborne Rangers model) and EU production (Ludo Fact).
