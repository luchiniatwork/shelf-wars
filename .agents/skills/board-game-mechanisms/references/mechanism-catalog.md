# Mechanism Catalog — Full Taxonomy Reference

Load this file when the user asks about a specific mechanism, wants exemplars to study, or needs per-mechanism player-count scaling detail.

Taxonomy sources: the BGG community mechanism tags (crowd-edited; ~51 entries cited in 2014, much larger today — treat any exact count as a snapshot [contested]) and the standard printed taxonomy: Engelstein & Shalev, *Building Blocks of Tabletop Game Design* (CRC Press, 2019) — ~196 mechanisms in 13 categories (Game Structures, Turn Order, Actions, Resolution, Game End & Victory, Uncertainty, Economics, etc.). Complementary analytic frames: Costikyan's 11 uncertainty sources (*Uncertainty in Games*, MIT Press 2013) and Fristoe's 8 system parts (Goal, Actions, Resources, Acquisition, Scoring, Elimination, Uncertainty, Interaction — League of Gamemakers, 2015).

Table columns: **Experience** = what it feels like · **Tension** = where the squeeze comes from · **Scaling** = behavior at low/high player counts · **Exemplars** (first/landmark first) · **Partners** = proven combination mechanisms.

## 1. Turn structure & action selection

| Mechanism | Experience | Tension | Scaling | Exemplars | Partners |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Worker placement** | Competition over scarce actions; being blocked hurts | You can never do everything; turn order is a resource | 1–5. Low counts soften contention; high counts increase blocking (Lords of Waterdeep gets *more* tense at 4–5p). 5–6p needs split boards or simultaneity | Keydom (1998, R. Breese — widely cited as first), Caylus (2005 — popularizer), Agricola (2007 — feeding pressure: 2 food/family member; spaces accumulate goods), Lords of Waterdeep (2012), Viticulture (2013 — Grande worker breaks blocking; summer/winter split boards) | Engine building, contracts, resource conversion |
| **Action points (AP)** | Freeform turn from a fixed budget | Many tempting options, few points | Any count, but AP-paralysis grows with option count | Tikal (1999 — 10 AP/turn), Pandemic (2008 — 4 actions/turn) | Almost anything; mitigate paralysis with sub-action menus, forced sequencing |
| **Action programming / queue** | Commit-then-resolve comedy & bluffing | Predicting others while locked in | Best 3–8; chaos scales with count | RoboRally (1994), Colt Express (2014), Mechs vs. Minions (2016 — damage locks program slots) | Simultaneous selection, modular board |
| **Simultaneous action selection** | Zero downtime; double-think | Decide without knowing others' choices | The default fix for 5+p downtime; downside: feels chaotic, needs conflict-resolution rules (Fristoe, "Game Elements: Actions") | Diplomacy (1959), 7 Wonders (2010), Race for the Galaxy role selection (2007) | Role selection, hidden roles, programming |
| **Rondel** | Wheel of actions; distance = cost | Future-denial: can't revisit cheap actions soon | Stable 2–5; low interaction unless shared | Antike (2005, Mac Gerdts — popularizer), Imperial (2006), Navegador (2010) | Economic engines, area control (Imperial) |
| **Mancala action selection** | Sow-and-resolve spatial planning | Counting ahead; cluster payoff timing | Stable 2–4 | Trajan (2011 — mancala × rondel hybrid), Five Tribes (2014), Istanbul (2014 — stack-movement variant) | Set collection, pick-up-and-deliver, modular board |

## 2. Growth & building

| Mechanism | Experience | Tension | Scaling | Exemplars | Partners |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Deck/bag/pool building** | You literally build your tool; growth fantasy | Curation vs. draw luck; dilution | Solitaire-friendly; low interaction → multiplayer-solitaire risk; add contested markets/boards | Dominion (2008 — genre origin, Vaccarino), Quarriors (2011 — dice pool), Orléans (2014 — bag), Clank! (2016 — × point-to-point board), Quacks of Quedlinburg (2018 — bag × push-your-luck) | Engine building, push-your-luck, route building, contracts |
| **Engine / tableau building** | Slow start → explosive end; visible machine | Invest vs. score now; watch rivals' engines | Any count; interaction must be designed in | Race for the Galaxy (2007), Splendor (2014), Terraforming Mars (2016), Wingspan (2019 — action-cube activation rows), Roll for the Galaxy (2014 — dice-as-resources) | Contracts, set collection, dice mitigation |
| **Tech trees** | Long-horizon asymmetric investment | Path commitment vs. flexibility | Best at 2–4 with long sessions | Civilization (1980), Through the Ages (2006; new ed. 2015), Gaia Project (2017) | Engine building, action points |

Failure mode shared by all three: **runaway leader** — needs scoring caps, catch-up, or a race trigger.

## 3. Distribution: drafting, auctions, hands, sets

| Mechanism | Experience | Tension | Scaling | Exemplars | Partners |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Card drafting** | Simultaneous hidden choice; reading opponents | Keep-vs-deny; you see a fraction of the pool | Best 4–6; thins at 2p (dummy hand or official 2p variant — 7 Wonders) | Traces to Magic: The Gathering booster draft (Richard Garfield, mid-1990s, inspired by US sports drafts — Nerdlab ep. 28). As the whole game: Sushi Go! (2013). As a between-rounds phase: Blood Rage (2015). 7 Wonders (2010) | Tableau/engine building, set collection. Requires interdependent card values or it's mere distribution (Nerdlab) |
| **Tile drafting** | Visible, deniable offers | Take what you need vs. deny theirs | Smooth 2–4 (factory offers scale) | Azul (2017), Kingdomino (2016 — draft order tied to tile quality) | Polyomino placement, pattern building |
| **Dice drafting** | Shared luck made fair | Pool composition vs. your window | Template: pool = 2n+1 dice for n players (5/7/9 at 2/3/4p) | Sagrada (2017) | Pattern building, dice mitigation |
| **Auctions — open/English** | Player-driven pricing; table energy | Valuation under competition | Needs 3+; stalls from "waiting out" | Modern Art (1992), Power Grid (2004 — passing locks you out of that plant's auction; one plant per player per round) | Network building, set collection |
| **Auctions — once-around** | Pressure in one lap | Bid enough now or lose it | Needs 3+; faster than open | Ra (1999 — sun discs are spent bids), Cyclades (2009 — one god won per player per round; outbid → immediately re-bid elsewhere) | Area control, hand management |
| **Auctions — sealed/blind** | Valuation prediction | No bluff-calling information | Needs 3+ | Revolution! (2009 — 3 currencies: gold, blackmail, force) | Area control, hidden roles |
| **Auctions — Dutch/descending** | Urgency; snap decisions | Price drops until someone bites | Needs 3+; favors fast deciders | Used inside many designs as a sub-phase | Markets, real-time play |
| **Hand management** | Few plays from many options | Timing: when to spend the good cards | Universal | Twilight Struggle (2005 — opponent-event cards), Hanamikoji (2013) | Partner to nearly everything |
| **Set collection** | Completion dopamine; visible progress | Shared scarcity, timing the cash-in | Universal; thin at 2p if pool small | Ticket to Ride (2004), Sushi Go! (2013), Splendor (2014) | Drafting, contracts, hand management |

Knizia's "auction trilogy": Modern Art (1992), Medici (1995), Ra (1999) — study all three for how format changes feel. Auction failure mode: novices can't value lots; Power Grid's demand-scaled resource market is the classic corrective.

## 4. Spatial play

| Mechanism | Experience | Tension | Scaling | Exemplars | Partners |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Area control / influence** | Visible map drama; territorial identity | Overextension vs. concentration; scoring-round timing | **3p problem:** two players over-contest one region, third sweeps the rest; best 4–5 | El Grande (1995 — landmark area-majority), Tigris & Euphrates (1997 — tile-grid influence), Web of Power (2000) / China (2005), Small World (2009 — deterministic conquest: 2 tokens for empty region + 1 per defensive piece), Rattus (2010 — inversion: sometimes you DON'T want majority). War-game branch: Risk (1959), Dune (1979) | Action points, card-driven actions, negotiation, route building |
| **Tile-laying** | A shared map emerges | Drawing what you need; exploiting others' placements | Smooth 2–5 | Carcassonne (2000), Isle of Skye (2015 — adds pricing your own tiles) | Area majority, set collection, network building |
| **Polyomino / pattern building** | Tetris-fit spatial puzzle | Shape scarcity vs. board space | Solitaire-leaning; add drafting for interaction | Patchwork (2014 — time-track economy), Bärenpark (2016), Isle of Cats (2019 — draft × polyomino × payment), Cascadia (2021 — pattern scoring; SdJ 2022) | Tile drafting, set collection |
| **Route / network building** | Connectivity optimization | Contested corridors, capacity | 2–5; map size should scale with count | Through the Desert (1998), Ticket to Ride (2004), Power Grid connection phase (2004), Brass (2007 — network IS the economy) | Pick-up-and-deliver, markets, area control |
| **Pick-up-and-deliver** | Logistical route efficiency | Capacity limits, delivery races | 2–5 | Himalaya (2002), Railroad Tycoon / Railways of the World (2005), Istanbul (2014) | Contracts, network building, engine upgrades |
| **Modular board** | Fresh map every game | Setup variance as replayability | Doubles as a scaling tool: board size ∝ player count | Catan (1995 hexes), Eclipse (2011) | Any spatial mechanism |

## 5. Luck & risk

| Mechanism | Experience | Tension | Scaling | Exemplars | Partners |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Push-your-luck** | Audible table drama; stop/continue | Probability vs. greed; players systematically misestimate bust odds | Universal; best with repeated small rounds (Incan Gold), not single big ones | Can't Stop (1980, Sackson), Incan Gold (2005 — simultaneous reveal adds group psychology), Martian Dice (2011), Quacks (2018) | Bag building, roll-and-write |
| **Roll-and-write / flip-and-write** | Simultaneous play, near-zero downtime, tiny component count | Committing random results to permanent grid; combo chaining | 1–many; Welcome To supports 1–100 | Yahtzee (1956 — ancestor: reroll mitigation), Qwixx (2012), Ganz schön clever (2018 — dice-selection cascade), Welcome To (2018 — flip-and-write, no dice), Cartographers (2020 — shared goals + ambush cards) | Pattern building, push-your-luck. Failure mode: multiplayer solitaire — fix via shared races or denial |
| **Dice mitigation** (technique family, not standalone) | Luck you can manage | — | — | Rerolls (Yahtzee; King of Tokyo 2011); ±pip modifiers (Castles of Burgundy 2011 workers); dice-as-resources not pass/fail (Alien Frontiers 2010, Euphoria 2013); roll-many-choose-subset; bell-curve aggregation (2d6: 7 = 6/36 ≈ 16.7% vs. flat d20); mitigation currencies; conversion powers (Grand Austria Hotel 2015); drafting from a shared pool (Sagrada) | Rule: prefer input luck; if output luck, price in reroll/modify/convert options |

## 6. Classic card-play traditions

| Mechanism | Experience | Tension | Scaling | Exemplars | Partners |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Trick-taking** | Micro-rounds, 20+ decisions per hand | Hand-depletion timing; reading voids | Usually 3–5; partnership variants at 4 | Renaissance: The Crew (2019 — cooperative trick-taking; Kennerspiel 2020), Cat in the Box; classics: Bridge, Skat | Cooperative constraints, bidding |
| **Climbing / ladder** | Shedding race with pattern recognition | Play now vs. wait for a better rung | 2–6 depending on design | Tichu (1991 — ancestor), Scout (2019 — can't rearrange your hand) | Hand management, betting |

## 7. Hidden information & social play

| Mechanism | Experience | Tension | Scaling | Exemplars | Partners |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hidden roles / social deduction** | Conversation-as-gameplay | Trust vs. suspicion | **Needs 5+**; The Resistance: 5–10 | Mafia/Werewolf (1986, Davidoff), The Resistance (2009), Avalon (2012 — asymmetric information fixes veteran meta) | Voting, traitor |
| **Traitor (in co-op frame)** | Paranoid cooperation | Help enough to win, hide enough to survive | 4–7 | Shadows over Camelot (2005 — first modern traitor), Battlestar Galactica (2008 — mid-game loyalty deal fixes early accusations), Dead of Winter (2014 — secret objectives soften pure traitor) | Semi-cooperative, hidden objectives. Pitfalls: eliminated players sit out; veteran meta dominates |
| **Semi-cooperative** | Shared loss, individual win | How much do I help? | 3–6 | Dead of Winter (2014), CO₂ (2012), New Angeles (2016) | Needs shared-loss enforcement or endgame kingmaking results |
| **Negotiation / trading** | Unscripted player economy | Deal-making vs. overpaying | 3–6; downtime and "alpha negotiator" risks | Bohnanza (1997 — fixed hand order forces trading), Chinatown (1999), Catan trading, Diplomacy | Markets, area control |
| **Voting** | Group judgment as resolution | Coalition math | Needs odd counts or tiebreakers | Werewolf eliminations, New Angeles (2016) | Hidden roles, negotiation |
| **Storytelling** | Constrained narrative generation | Creativity under constraint | Party counts | Tales of the Arabian Nights (1985), Once Upon a Time (1993) | Low strategy tolerance; pair with light scoring |
| **Deduction** | Information triangulation | Asking the right question first | 2–5 | Clue (1949), Alchemists (2014 — app-checked logic grid), Cryptid (2018 — honest-information rules) | Hand management, asymmetric info |
| **Memory** | Recall as skill | Imperfect information | Universal | Pure: Memory. Embedded: Hanabi (2010 — inverted: you see everyone's hand but your own) | Accessibility caveat — see `board-game-accessibility` |
| **Dexterity** | Physical skill as resolution | Nerves under pressure | Party/family counts | Crokinole, Catacombs (2010), Ascending Empires (2011 — dexterity × area control), Flick 'em Up (2015) | Polarizing; accessibility caveat — never gate core scoring purely on it for family targets |

## 8. Economics

| Mechanism | Experience | Tension | Scaling | Exemplars | Partners |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Market / speculation** | Value manipulation; timing exits | Buy low, sell before the crash | 3–5 typical | Acquire (1964, Sackson), Container (2007 — closed player economy), Stockpile (2015 — insider information), Power Grid resource market (price scales with demand) | Network building, auctions |
| **Contracts** | Quests as score funnels | Which contracts to chase; engine output → goals | Universal | Lords of Waterdeep (2012), Century: Spice Road (2017), Wingspan bonus cards (2019) | Engine building, worker placement, pick-up-and-deliver |

## 9. Meta-structure & long-form

| Mechanism | Experience | Tension | Scaling | Exemplars | Partners |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Legacy / unlockable content** | Permanent consequence converts decisions into narrative | Choices echo across sessions | Fixed group; one group per copy (replayability ceiling) | Risk Legacy (2011, Daviau — genre origin; deface/destroy components), Pandemic Legacy (2015, Daviau & Leacock — 12 in-game months × ≤2 attempts = 12–24 sessions; BGG #1 for years), Charterstone (2017, Stegmaier — playable post-campaign state), Gloomhaven (2017 — envelope/character unlocks, no destruction) | Design needs: paced reveals (sealed envelopes), meaningful permanence, decision-tree cost |
| **Campaign systems** | Connected multi-session arc | Persistent state between sessions | Same-group constraint | Pandemic Legacy, Gloomhaven, Charterstone | Stegmaier definition: "designed to be played over multiple sessions with connecting narrative and persistent elements," with micro-endings (episode cleanup) and macro-endings (overarching goal) |
| **Tug-of-war tracks** | Zero-sum bipolar meters concentrate conflict | Every gain is opponent's loss | Best at exactly 2 players or 2 teams | Twilight Struggle (2005 — influence), 13 Days (2016), Watergate (2022 — momentum track) | Card-driven actions, hand management, asymmetric powers |

## Scaling levers (apply to any mechanism)

- **Linear shared pools**: Sagrada's 2n+1 dice per round — template for any shared offer.
- **Modular board size ∝ player count**: Catan, Eclipse.
- **Per-count setup cards**: Agricola and 7 Wonders "3+" / "4+" cards gate content by count.
- **Split seasonal boards**: Viticulture separates summer/winter worker spaces to preserve contention across counts.
- **Simultaneity**: the default downtime fix at 5+p (7 Wonders, roll-and-writes).
- **Dummy players/variants**: the default 2p fix for drafting (7 Wonders official variant).

## Exemplar study list (canonical failures & fixes)

- Worker placement → **Agricola** (feeding pressure as tension amplifier), **Viticulture** (Grande worker = blocking relief valve)
- Deck building → **Dominion** (the origin), **Clank!** (how to add a board), **Quacks** (how to add push-your-luck)
- Drafting → **7 Wonders** (pure card draft), **Blood Rage** (draft as between-rounds phase), **Sagrada** (dice draft + scaling math)
- Area majority → **El Grande** (the landmark), **Rattus** (the inversion), **Small World** (deterministic conquest math)
- Auctions → **Modern Art / Medici / Ra** (Knizia trilogy: three formats, three feels), **Power Grid** (market correction)
- Engine building → **Race for the Galaxy** (simultaneity), **Wingspan** (action-cube activation)
- Push-your-luck → **Can't Stop** (the ancestor), **Incan Gold** (simultaneous group psychology)
- Legacy → **Risk Legacy** (permanence), **Pandemic Legacy** (paced reveals), **Charterstone** (post-campaign playability)
- Traitor → **Shadows over Camelot** (first modern), **Battlestar Galactica** (mid-game loyalty fix)
- Tug-of-war → **Twilight Struggle** (the 2p benchmark), **Watergate** (asymmetric tug-of-war)
