# Mechanism Combinations, Core Loops & Innovation

Load this file when combining mechanisms, designing a core loop from scratch, hunting for mechanical novelty, or auditing a design for bloat.

## 1. The four jobs a supporting mechanism can do

A supporting mechanism is legitimate only if it does at least one of these for the core:

1. **Feeds the core's inputs** — Agricola's farming spaces feed workers (family growth) into the worker-placement core.
2. **Consumes the core's outputs** — Lords of Waterdeep's contracts consume the cube output of worker placement and funnel it into goals.
3. **Covers the core's weakness** — Clank! adds a point-to-point board to deck-building to fix multiplayer solitaire; Quacks' push-your-luck adds table drama to bag building.
4. **Contrasts the rhythm** — Blood Rage's calm drafting phase alternates with explosive battle rounds; Feast for Odin's quiet puzzle turns alternate with scarce worker-placement rounds.

If a subsystem does none of these, it is bloat (see §6).

## 2. Proven synergy patterns

| Pattern | Why it works | Exemplars |
|---|---|---|
| Building × risk | Growth engine with a bust valve creates drama per draw | Quacks of Quedlinburg (2018, bag × push-your-luck); The Castles of Burgundy (2011, dice × conversion powers) |
| Drafting × building | The draft IS the economy; picks compound into an engine | 7 Wonders (2010), Blood Rage (2015), Isle of Cats (2019, draft × polyomino × payment), Sagrada (2017) |
| Engine × contracts | Engine produces; contracts aim output at goals | Wingspan (2019), Lords of Waterdeep (2012), Century: Spice Road (2017) |
| Network × economy | The map is the market; routes are revenue | Brass (2007), Power Grid (2004: route × auction × demand-scaled resource market) |
| Worker placement × auction | One mechanism prices the other's scarcity | Keyflower (2012) |
| Worker placement × upkeep pressure | A feeding obligation forces engine growth | Agricola (2007 — 2 food per family member per harvest) |
| Tug-of-war × asymmetric card-driven actions | Zero-sum track + asymmetric decks = concentrated 2p conflict | Twilight Struggle (2005), Watergate (2022) |
| Mancala × rondel | Two cycle-geometries interlock; planning = counting | Trajan (2011) |
| Dice-as-workers × worker placement | Luck enters as INPUT (place what you rolled), preserving agency | Alien Frontiers (2010), Euphoria (2013) |
| Bag building × map | Builder output is spent on geography | Orléans (2014) |
| Driving mechanism × board play | A turn-engine (roles/cards/wheel) drives map consequences; design both in parallel, each constrains the other (League of Gamemakers, "Parallel Design") | Puerto Rico role selection (2002), Trajan mancala-rondel, 7 Wonders drafting, Deities & Demigods (Jaffee, deity-deck driving board combat/quests) |
| Trick-taking × co-op | A competitive tradition inverted into shared fate | The Crew (2019; Kennerspiel 2020) |

## 3. Core-loop recipes (skeletons to adapt)

Every recipe must close the loop: **generate → convert → score**. Currencies: earnable, spendable, scarce.

**Euro engine loop** (Splendor, Wingspan)
`take resources → buy/place engine parts → engine produces more per turn → convert surplus into points/bonus cards`
Race trigger ends the game before engines fully mature (anti-runaway).

**Deck-builder loop** (Dominion, Clank!)
`draw hand → spend on new cards (thin weak cards) → reshuffled deck is stronger → repeat until a depletion/race trigger`
Add a board or monsters (Clank!) to create contestable space.

**Worker-placement loop** (Agricola, Viticulture)
`place workers on contested spaces → harvest resources → invest in engine/upkeep → feed obligations → score rounds`
Turn order becomes a currency; blocking IS the interaction.

**Draft-and-tableau loop** (7 Wonders, Blood Rage)
`simultaneous pick → pass → build → resolve a scoring/conflict phase → draft again`
Draft quality depends on card interdependency (a pick must change remaining values — Nerdlab).

**Social-deduction loop** (The Resistance, Avalon)
`information drip → proposal/discussion → vote → mission/quest result updates suspicion → repeat to majority condition`
Needs 5+ players and asymmetric information to survive veteran metas.

**Roll-and-write loop** (Qwixx, Ganz schön clever)
`shared roll → simultaneous commit to grid → combos chain → locked rows force endgame`
Fix multiplayer solitaire with shared races or shared goals (Cartographers).

## 4. Innovation playbook

Novelty comes from three moves plus one wildcard. First-to-market with a genuinely big idea dominates the category (League of Gamemakers, "The Next Big Idea in Tabletop Gaming": D&D, Magic: The Gathering, Dominion each spawned industries of spinoffs).

### Recombination — proven mechanism × new substrate
Procedure: take a mechanism whose decision-feel you want; attach it to a substrate (board, campaign, theme-space) it hasn't colonized.
- Deck-building × point-to-point board → **Clank!** (2016)
- Bag building × push-your-luck → **Quacks of Quedlinburg** (2018)
- Trick-taking × cooperative play → **The Crew** (2019)
- Worker placement × auction pricing → **Keyflower** (2012)
- Legacy permanence × co-op campaign → **Pandemic Legacy** (2015)

### Inversion — flip the genre default
Procedure: list the mechanism's default assumptions ("you see your own hand", "majorities are good", "you play against the others"); invert exactly one.
- See everyone else's hand, never your own → **Hanabi** (2010)
- Sometimes you DON'T want the majority → **Rattus** (2010)
- Anti-colonizer area control (play the defenders) → **Spirit Island** (2017)
- Competitive tradition played cooperatively → **The Crew** (2019)

### Constraint change — remove or tighten one degree of freedom
Procedure: pick a degree of freedom players take for granted (reorder your hand, player-count independence, component permanence); remove it and design around the hole.
- Cannot rearrange your hand → **Scout** (2019); fixed hand order forces trading → **Bohnanza** (1997)
- Remove dice from the Yahtzee formula → flip-and-write, 1–100 players → **Welcome To** (2018)
- Draft order bound to tile quality (better tile = later pick next round) → **Kingdomino** (2016)
- Shared dice pool sized 2n+1 → **Sagrada** (2017)

### New substrate / technology (wildcard)
- Permanence & component destruction → **Risk Legacy** (2011, Daviau — origin of the legacy genre)
- Sealed envelopes / unlockables without destruction → **Gloomhaven** (2017)
- App-checked hidden logic → **Alchemists** (2014)
- Post-campaign playable state → **Charterstone** (2017)

### Reusing your own mechanisms (legitimate)
When a mechanism has untapped potential, re-deploy it in a new frame: Matt Leacock reused Pandemic's infection deck (shuffle the discard pile onto the deck after each Epidemic card, in the Intensify step — threat concentrates in known hotspots while order stays hidden) in Forbidden Island (League of Gamemakers, "Cannibalizing Mechanics"). Reuse is fine when the new context generates different decisions; it's lazy when only the theme changed.

## 5. Mechanism-count discipline (elegance vs. bloat)

- **Budget:** 1 core + 2–3 supporting mechanisms [contested — community consensus, no named originator]. The true constraint is depth-to-complexity ratio, not the number.
- **Knizia:** "Simple games, but then the people bring themselves into it… out of the simplicity, a second level of depth."
- **Saint-Exupéry** (the most-quoted bloat antidote in design blogs): "Perfection is achieved not when there is nothing more to add, but when there is nothing left to take away."
- **Stegmaier red flags:** playtest feedback containing "remember…" or "except…" signals rules weight that isn't paying for itself. His tenets push "elegant, streamlined core games that have incredible depth and complexity."
- **Documented cut (precedent):** Geoff Engelstein's *The Expanse* postmortem [unverified — cited in designer discussions, source not locatable] — cut faction goals, an economy system, and a tension track because they "pushed the game past the complexity we wanted for the target audience" without adding interest.

### Bloat audit procedure
1. List every subsystem. For each: which core-loop input does it feed, or output does it consume? (§1 jobs.) No answer → cut.
2. Count "remember/except" rules per system. Highest count → simplify or cut first.
3. Time the teach. Over the audience's tolerance → remove systems before simplifying them (a simplified extra system is still extra).
4. Check each subsystem's decision frequency: a mechanism that fires only a few times per game must justify itself with very high drama per firing.
5. Verify ONE primary randomizer. Each additional luck source needs mitigation tech (reroll/modify/convert) or it compounds swinginess.
6. Verify ONE scoring funnel (two max, one a loss condition).

### Anti-patterns
| Anti-pattern | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Two cores fighting | Players ask "which game am I playing?"; split attention | Demote one core to supporting role or split into two designs |
| Bolt-on minigame | A phase with its own rules island, unrelated inputs/outputs | Cut it, or wire its outputs into the main loop |
| Double primary randomizer | Outcomes feel arbitrary; skill complaints | Keep one randomizer; convert the other to mitigation tech |
| Rarely-firing subsystem | Forgotten rules; "oh right, that exists" | Cut, or make it fire every round |
| Interaction painted on | Engine game + one attack card | Design interaction into the economy (shared markets, races, drafting) |
| Catch-up as separate system | A rubber-band rule bolted on late | Prefer structural caps: race triggers, scoring ceilings, depletion clocks |

## 6. Quick pairing FAQ

- "My engine game feels solitaire" → add a shared race, a shared market, drafting, or a contestable board (Clank! move).
- "My drafting game is flat" → check card interdependency; if picks don't revalue remaining cards, it's distribution, not drafting (Nerdlab).
- "Auctions stall / new players misprice" → once-around format (Ra), single-god bids (Cyclades), or a demand-scaled market (Power Grid).
- "Too much downtime at 5–6p" → simultaneous selection (7 Wonders) or single-action turns (Fristoe's downtime mitigation).
- "2p drafting feels thin" → dummy hand or official 2p variant; or switch substrate to factory drafting (Azul).
- "Dice feel unfair" → move luck to input side: dice-as-resources (Alien Frontiers), pip modifiers (Castles of Burgundy), shared draft pool (Sagrada).
