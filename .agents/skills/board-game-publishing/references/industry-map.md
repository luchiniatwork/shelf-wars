# Industry Map — Conventions, Media, Contests, Portals, BGG

Load-trigger: planning convention strategy, choosing reviewers/previews, finding contests or submission portals, or navigating BoardGameGeek.

Attendance figures below are organizer-reported, verified via Wikipedia/ICv2 at research time; always re-check before planning travel.

**Etiquette that is universal:** never cold-pitch a booth mid-show (staff are selling); book meetings weeks ahead; carry one-sheets (see `sell-sheet.md`), not prototypes; follow up within a week.

**Running a demo table (exhibitor norms):** keep demos to 10–20 minutes of play, not a full game — table turnover is the metric; run scheduled full games through the show's event catalog (ticketed events fill tables and feed the BGG ratings pipeline); never leave a waiting prospect unacknowledged; respect trade/press hours and don't bleed into a neighbor's frontage or the aisle; budget 2–3 demo copies per table per show — copies wear out, walk away, and lose components.

**Selling at the show (booth economics):** direct sales keep full price per unit, but the real cost is booth + badges + travel + lodging + stock shipping/drayage + ~3% card fees. Break-even test: units × (price − landed cost − fees) ≥ total show cost. Stock 1.5–2× an honest unit forecast — sell-outs lose margin, unsold stock ships home at your cost. Convention selling beats distribution when the release is new, demo-able, and your audience knows you'll be there; distribution still wins for catalog depth and year-round reach (channel margin table: `board-game-market-analysis`).

## 2. Designer-development events (go here first)

- **Protospiel** — the original playtest-everyone's-prototype format; regional events plus **Protospiel Online** (since 2020). Publishers and veteran designers attend.
- **Unpub** — "feedback festival" (Baltimore area) plus chapter events; structured public playtesting with feedback forms; publisher scouting.
- **Metatopia** — Double Exposure's design-focused convention (Morristown, NJ, Nov): panels, playtesting, publisher seminars.
- **Publisher speed dating** — timed 3–5-minute pitches to a rotation of publishers; recurring at Origins, Gen Con, Dice Tower Con. Slots fill early; research which publishers attend before buying a badge.
- **Stonemaier Design Day** — annual open event; Stonemaier scouts games designers bring (they explicitly note they look at games there, though they don't hear pitches).

## 3. Reviewer / preview ecosystem

| Outlet | Format | Payment policy (as stated by outlet) |
|---|---|---|
| Shut Up & Sit Down | Written + video reviews | Accepts no money for coverage (Patreon-supported) |
| The Dice Tower (Tom Vasel) | Video reviews, previews | Kickstarter previews are paid, labeled marketing; reviews unpaid |
| Rahdo Runs Through (Richard Ham) | Filmed playthroughs | Paid runthroughs, explicitly *not* reviews |
| Watch It Played (Rodney Smith) | How-to-play videos | Sponsored teaching videos, disclosed |
| Heavy Cardboard | Heavy-strategy playthroughs/podcast | Sponsorships disclosed |
| Space-Biff (Dan Thurot) | Written criticism | Review copies; no paid coverage |
| No Pun Included / Actualol | Video reviews/essays | No paid reviews; sponsorships disclosed |
| Man vs Meeple, Meeple Mountain, Board Game Quest, The Opinionated Gamers | Previews + reviews (video/written) | Mixed; previews commonly paid, reviews copy-only |

**The paid-preview debate, settled practice:** previews, runthroughs, and how-to-play videos are advertising — paying is standard *if disclosed*. Reviews are criticism — payment destroys the outlet's credibility and yours. Budget previews as a marketing line (see `board-game-crowdfunding`); never present a paid preview as a review; never ask a reviewer to trade coverage for money.

## 4. Contests and credibility

| Contest | What it is | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Cardboard Edison Award | Annual, unpublished games only, judged by industry professionals (2026 winner: *Astrolabe* by Yasaman Farazan) | The strongest line-item on a sell sheet's status line |
| Ion Award (SaltCON) | Design competition with publisher judges, strategy/family categories | Publisher meetings are built in |
| BGG community design contests | Rolling PnP contests (24-hour, mint-tin, 18-card, solitaire, etc.), community-judged | Fast iteration reps + a winnable credential |
| Unpub/Protospiel events | Not contests, but logged public playtesting | The "blind-tested N sessions" evidence publishers ask for |

## 5. Submission portals and how to work them

- **Find them:** every mid-size publisher has a submissions page or form (example verified: Stonemaier — form requiring sell sheet, visual overview and/or short video; ~1-month response; no unsolicited prototypes; $10,000 advance on signing). Cardboard Edison curates designer-resource links, including publisher submission info.
- **Mass market is closed:** Hasbro, Mattel, Wizards of the Coast accept nothing unsolicited; they work through toy-industry agents (who take 40–50% of inventor royalties — Tinsman).
- **Windows:** portals open and close; log submission date, contact, asset versions, and the stated response window; one follow-up, then move on; parallel submissions are accepted practice.

## 6. BGG ecosystem reality

- **Forums that matter for designers:** Board Game Design forum, Works-in-Progress threads, the design-contest forums, guilds. Post designer diaries from your game's entry page; WIP threads build the first 100 fans.
- **Ranking reality:** BGG ranks on a Bayesian average — dummy votes pull low-volume games toward the middle, so a game needs substantial ratings volume before its "true" score moves the rank (exact dummy-vote parameters have changed over time [contested]). Practical consequence: chase *number of ratings* (convention demos, PnP communities, TTS mods), not a few perfect 10s; the Hotness list is visit-driven, not quality-driven.
- **What BGG is for pre-publication:** credibility (contest wins, diary traffic), community (co-designers, playtesters), and market research (see `board-game-market-analysis`) — not direct sales.
