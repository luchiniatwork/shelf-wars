# Sell Sheet, Video Pitch, Pitch Email & Box Back Copy — Annotated Templates

Load-trigger: the user wants to write, review, or fix a sell sheet, video pitch, pitch email, speed-dating one-sheet, or retail-facing box back copy.

The sell sheet is the designer's resume (Cardboard Edison's framing: the sell sheet is your resume, the rulebook is your cover letter). Publishers decide whether to ask for more in under 60 seconds. One page. PDF. No attachments beyond it in first contact.

---

## 1. Sell sheet anatomy (one page, top to bottom)

| Zone | Contents | Hard rules |
|---|---|---|
| Header | Game title + logline | Logline ≤15 words: who you are + what you do + the twist |
| Hero | Key art, logo, or one clean component render/photo | No cluttered table shots; readability at thumbnail size |
| Stat strip | Players · Age · Time · Weight | Weight as light/medium/heavy (or BGG-style 1–5); match publisher's line |
| Body bullets | 3–5 bullets | One idea per bullet: hook mechanism, core tension, session arc, why-now/market fit |
| Components | Counts only | "150 cards, 60 wooden tokens, 4 dual-layer boards, 1 dice tower" — no art direction essay |
| Footer | Designer name, contact, links, status | Links: video pitch, rules PDF, optional TTS mod; status line with playtest evidence |

### What each element must do

- **Logline** — survives the 10-second test. Formula: *[player fantasy] + [core verb/mechanism] + [unique twist]*. Weak: "A fun strategy game for the whole family." Strong: "Rival lighthouse keepers draft fog banks to wreck — or save — the same ships."
- **Stat strip** — the publisher's first filter. If their line is 2–4 player, 60–90 min, $40–60 games, your strip must say so in one glance. Never pad player counts you haven't blind-tested (Stonemaier rejects games outside their published count/length wants without reading further).
- **Bullets** — mechanisms named precisely (deck-building, worker placement, action drafting), tension source stated, and one bullet that answers "why does this exist in 2026's market?" (comparable titles + the gap).
- **Component counts** — publishers cost the game mentally from this line. A $30-box game with 400 cards and minis is a red flag before they read bullet one (cost realism: see `board-game-manufacturing`).
- **Status line** — proof of diligence: "Blind-playtested 45 sessions across 6 groups; rules-stable since March." Publishers rank playtest depth above art (Stonemaier's guidelines make polish/playtesting a hard requirement and explicitly ban art investment in prototypes).

### One-page discipline

- Readable in <60 seconds; nothing longer than two lines.
- No rules teaching — teach nothing; evoke everything.
- No design history ("I started this in 2019 with my kids…") — nobody buys your journey on page one.
- No walls of text, no more than 2 fonts, no watermarked stock art.
- Export as PDF, <5 MB, named `GameTitle_SellSheet_YourName.pdf`.

## 2. Worked example (fictional game, shown for shape only)

```
TIDEWATER                                        [hero art: harbor at dusk, crane silhouettes]
Rival harbor masters draft tide cards to load — or strand — the same ships.

2–5 players  ·  Ages 10+  ·  60–90 min  ·  Medium weight

• Tide-lock drafting: every card you take sets the tide your neighbors must obey
• One shared harbor: loading your ship advances rivals' cargo timers — help, hurt, or both
• 45-minute arc from sleepy port to frantic simultaneous storm round
• Sits in the "interactive engine-builder" slot next to [Comparable A] and [Comparable B],
  at a $45 component footprint

150 cards · 84 wooden goods tokens · 5 player boards · 1 harbor board

J. Designer — j@example.com — tidewater.example
Video pitch (4 min) · Rules PDF · TTS mod on request
Blind-playtested 45 sessions, 6 groups; finalist, [contest name]
```

Why this works: logline gives fantasy + verb + twist in 15 words; strip matches a real publisher slot; bullets name mechanisms, tension, arc, and market gap; components cost the box honestly; footer carries evidence, not autobiography.

## 3. Video pitch script (3–8 minutes)

Stonemaier's submission form asks for "a short video highlighting a few turns and your favorite mechanisms" — this is the industry template. Shoot horizontal, decent light, phone camera acceptable; edit nothing fancy.

| Time | Segment | Script beats |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | Hook | Logline verbatim from the sell sheet; one sentence on who it's for |
| 0:30–1:00 | Component spread | Slow pan; call out counts; show the table footprint |
| 1:00–4:30 | Real turns | Play 2–3 actual turns narrating decisions ("I could X, but then she'll Y…") — decisions, not rules recitation |
| 4:30–5:30 | Unique + fit | The one thing no published game does; name the publisher's line slot it fills ("this sits between your A and B") |
| 5:30–6:00 | CTA | Status (playtest depth), what you're asking for (meeting, prototype request), contact |

Sins: reading the rulebook on camera, monetization talk ("this will sell 10,000 units"), apologizing for prototype art, exceeding the publisher's stated length cap.

## 4. Pitch email / submission-form answers

Three sentences plus links; no attachments unless the form requires them.

1. **Logline + stat strip, verbatim** — consistency across assets signals professionalism.
2. **Why this publisher** — one specific, true sentence: "Your line has no 5-player interactive engine-builder under $50; Tidewater is built for that slot."
3. **Status + links** — playtest evidence, contest results, video link, sell-sheet PDF link, rules PDF link.

Then stop. Follow up once after the publisher's stated response window (Stonemaier: ~1 month). No reply after follow-up = move on; parallel submissions are accepted practice (Stonemaier states it explicitly).

## 5. Speed-dating one-sheet variant (convention)

Same page, rebalanced for a 3–5 minute table slot: logline and stat strip larger; cut to 3 bullets; add a QR code to the video; print 20 copies. Leave-behind only — you keep talking while they read. Sign up for speed-dating slots early (Origins, Gen Con, Dice Tower Con — they fill fast), and prioritize the publishers whose line fits rather than the biggest names.

## 6. Box back copy (retail-facing)

The box back is the only salesperson a game has on a store shelf. Publishers write their own on licensed games (sometimes consulting the designer); self-publishers must write it. Distinct from box-bottom regulatory markings (UPC, age label, origin, addresses) — those are compliance → `board-game-manufacturing` (component-specs box-markings checklist).

| Zone | Contents | Hard rules |
|---|---|---|
| Hook paragraph | 2–4 sentences: player fantasy + core tension + twist | Written for a buyer holding the box in a store, not a rules reader; no mechanism glossary |
| Feature bullets | 3–5 bullets naming mechanisms and experience | One idea per bullet; mirror the sell sheet's claims so marketing stays consistent |
| Contents list | Exact component counts ("150 cards, 84 wooden tokens…") | Must match the manufactured contents exactly — mismatches trigger retail returns |
| Quotes | 1–3 short attributed quotes | Real, attributable, unpaid; never quote a paid preview as if it were a review (see `industry-map.md` §3) |
| Icon strip | Players · age · time, in icons | Must match the sell-sheet stat strip and manufacturing's box-bottom markings |
| Footer | Publisher logo, URL/QR, social handle | QR to a 60-second how-it-plays video converts browsers |

Panel rules: teach nothing, evoke everything (same discipline as the sell sheet); every superlative needs evidence; the player count and time printed here are a promise — they must match the game as manufactured.
