# Board Game Publishing — Routes, Pitching & the Industry

Publishing is a business decision with three doors: license the design (sell rights, take royalties), self-publish (keep rights, take all the risk), or work-for-hire (sell your time). Your job: route the user to the right door, then make their pitch assets, contract position, and industry calendar match reality.

## When to use / when not to use

Use this skill when the user needs to:
- Choose between licensing, self-publishing (usually crowdfunding), and work-for-hire — with realistic economics of each.
- Build pitch assets: sell sheet, short video pitch, pitch email, submission-form answers.
- Target publishers, work submission portals and convention speed dating, handle pitch etiquette.
- Evaluate a publishing contract: royalty rate and base, advance, term, reversion, rights scope, credit.
- Navigate conventions, designer events (Unpub, Protospiel, Metatopia), BGG, reviewers/previews, design contests.
- Calibrate expectations: designer earnings, 18–24-month contract-to-shelf timelines, name-on-box credit.

Do NOT use this skill for:
- Running the crowdfunding campaign itself (pre-launch funnel, pledge tiers, stretch goals, pledge managers, VAT/shipping) → `board-game-crowdfunding`.
- Quotes, MOQ, print specs, freight, compliance → `board-game-manufacturing`.
- Comparable-title pricing and distribution-channel economics depth → `board-game-market-analysis`.
- Getting the design pitch-ready (it must be fully playable and blind-playtested first) → `board-game-playtesting`, `board-game-rules-writing`.
- Licensing someone else's IP for your game's theme → `board-game-theme-narrative`.

## Core principles

1. **Ideas are worthless; executed games are the currency.** Stonemaier's submission guidelines say it verbatim: "Ideas are important but largely worthless." Publishers buy fully created, extensively blind-playtested, rules-ready games. Stonemaier's #1 reported mistake: prototypes that are literally unplayable. Never pitch an unproven design.
2. **The publisher is the customer of your pitch.** Read each publisher's submission page as a spec sheet (Stonemaier publishes exact wants: event games of 1–2 hours, 2→5+ players, unique mechanism, no historical/religious themes, no campaign/legacy). Pitch only games that fill an open slot in *their* line — player count, weight, price point, theme — and say so explicitly.
3. **A pitch is an asset stack, not a package in the mail.** Sell sheet (one page) → 3–8-minute video → rules PDF → prototype *only on request*. Never mail an unsolicited prototype: it violates every publisher's stated process (Stonemaier: "Do not mail a prototype to us unless we request it") and signals amateurism.
4. **Royalties are a small cut of someone else's revenue.** Standard hobby royalty: 5–8% of the publisher's *net* receipts, observed range 2–10% (Tom Jolly, League of Gamemakers). With distribution, net ≈ 40% of MSRP, so 6% of net ≈ 2.4% of MSRP ≈ $1.20/unit on a $50 game. Do this math before choosing a route.
5. **The killer clauses are termination and reversion.** Jolly: publishers sometimes never publish, yet hold rights for years; one POD print run of 100 copies has been used to claim "publication" and renew a term. Demand a fixed termination date and a minimum-print threshold.
6. **Advances are rare and small — one public exception.** Jolly: a few hundred to $5,000, recouped against royalties. Stonemaier publicly offers a $10,000 advance on signing — treat it as the exception that proves how unusual real money up front is.
7. **Publishers bet on flexible designers.** They will change your game. Stonemaier: "If you won't consider any changes, you're not a good fit for us." Declare up front the one or two things you won't change; be negotiable on everything else.
8. **Conventions are for relationships, not cold booth pitches.** Publisher staff are selling, not scouting, at big shows. Use designer events (Unpub, Protospiel, Metatopia), publisher speed dating (Origins, Gen Con, Dice Tower Con), and scheduled meetings. Pitches are won in the follow-up email.
9. **Previews are paid marketing; reviews are not.** Paying for labeled previews/runthroughs (Dice Tower, Rahdo) is standard crowdfunding marketing. Reviews should be unpaid (Shut Up & Sit Down accepts no money for coverage). Never pay for a "review"; never hide that a preview was paid.
10. **Plan in years, not months.** Contract-to-shelf is commonly 18–24 months (publisher development, art, manufacturing, freight). Self-publishing via crowdfunding adds roughly 12–18 months of pre-launch, campaign, production, and fulfillment. Say no route is faster without saying what gets skipped.
11. **Design is portfolio income, not a salary.** Community consensus and designer surveys consistently show most published designers earn hobby money (<$10k/year) [contested exact figures]; a few hundred people worldwide do it full-time. Advise keeping the day job until royalty history says otherwise.
12. **Your name becomes an asset — protect credit from game #1.** German publishers pioneered designer-name-on-box (Reiner Knizia built a brand on it); the hobby industry has followed. Negotiate credit placement in the contract, not after.

## How to apply it

### A. Choose the publishing route

Walk the user through four questions: (1) Do you have $15k–$40k+ you can genuinely lose? (2) Do you want to run a business — marketing, freight, fulfillment, customer service, forming an entity, carrying product-liability insurance — or design games? (3) Is the game's MSRP economics viable at 1,500–2,500 units? (4) Do you already have an audience?

| Route | You invest | You keep | You earn | Best when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| License to publisher | Time only | Royalties + credit | 5–8% of net (Jolly); rare advance | You want to design, not operate |
| Self-publish (crowdfund) | $15k–$40k+, 1–2 years, entity + liability insurance | All rights, all margin | 10–20% net margin if disciplined [approximate] | You have an audience and operator appetite |
| Work-for-hire | Time | Usually nothing but the fee | Flat fee, wide range [contested] | Building a track record; licensed-IP gigs |

Self-publishing means running a small consumer-products company: form an entity (LLC or local equivalent), carry product-liability insurance (distributors, 3PLs, and some shows demand certificates), and plan taxes on campaign funds and direct sales — checklist in `references/deal-economics.md` (not tax or legal advice).

Full worked economics: load `references/deal-economics.md`.

### B. Build the pitch asset stack

1. **Sell sheet** — one page, readable in <60 seconds: title + logline (≤15 words), key art or hero component shot, players/age/time/weight strip, 3–5 bullets (hook mechanism, tension, arc, market fit), component counts, designer + contact + links. Full annotated template: load `references/sell-sheet.md`.
2. **Video pitch** — 3–8 minutes: hook (30s) → component spread (30s) → 2–3 real turns (3–4 min) → what's unique + why it fits *this* publisher (1 min) → CTA. Stonemaier's form asks for exactly "a short video highlighting a few turns and your favorite mechanisms."
3. **Rules PDF** — blind-playtested; confusing writing and typos "will negatively affect our impression" (Stonemaier). Route rulebook quality through `board-game-rules-writing`.
4. **Prototype on request only** — functional graphic design (UI clarity), zero art investment. Stonemaier: "please do not invest in [art and graphic design] for a submission prototype."
5. **Optional accelerants** — Tabletop Simulator mod, PnP file, contest results, playtest logs (session counts prove diligence: "blind-tested 40+ sessions").
6. **Box back copy** (retail-facing; publishers write their own on licensed games — self-publishers must) — the back panel is the shelf salesperson: hook paragraph, feature bullets, contents list, quotes, icon strip. Template: load `references/sell-sheet.md`. Box-bottom regulatory markings are a compliance matter → `board-game-manufacturing`.

### C. Target and submit

1. Build a target list of 5–15 publishers whose current line has a slot your game fills (check their last 2 years of releases; Cardboard Edison maintains publisher-resource links).
2. Check each publisher's submission status (many open/close windows; Stonemaier accepts via form with ~1-month response; mass-market giants like Hasbro/Mattel/Wizards accept *nothing* unsolicited and work via toy-industry agents only).
3. Submit per each publisher's exact spec — never a generic blast.
4. Parallel-submit unless a publisher explicitly requests exclusivity (Stonemaier: "We won't be offended if you submit your game to other publishers in the meantime").
5. Log everything: date, contact, asset versions, response. Follow up once after the stated response window, then move on.

### D. Work conventions and designer events

- **Designer-development events first**: Protospiel (regional + Protospiel Online), Unpub (Baltimore), Metatopia (NJ) — feedback and publisher scouting in low-pressure settings.
- **Speed dating**: sign up early (slots fill) at Origins, Gen Con, Dice Tower Con; 3–5 minutes per publisher; lead with the logline, leave the sell sheet.
- **Big consumer shows** (Gen Con, Essen, UKGE): meetings by appointment only; never cold-pitch a booth mid-show.
- **Running a demo table** — short demos, table turnover, scheduled events, demo-copy wear: norms in `references/industry-map.md`.
- **Selling at the show** — booth cost vs margin math, stock sizing, when convention selling beats distribution: worked numbers in `references/industry-map.md`.
- Convention table with verified attendance: load `references/industry-map.md`.

### E. Evaluate an offer or contract

Check, in order: royalty rate AND base (net receipts vs MSRP vs "profit" — demand net receipts, Jolly's standard), advance and recoupment, term length and renewal conditions, reversion (fixed date; out-of-print = rights revert; bar POD-only "publication" or require a minimum print run, e.g. 1,000 copies — Jolly's loophole), territory/languages, expansion and digital rights, modification rights, credit and name/likeness use, payment cadence (quarterly or semiannual is standard) with audit rights. Then have a contract lawyer read it. Full clause checklist: load `references/deal-economics.md`.

### F. Navigate the ecosystem

- **BGG**: maintain the game's entry early (designer diary, WIP forum thread), enter community contests; understand that rankings use a Bayesian average with dummy votes, so small rating counts can't crack ranked lists — chase ratings volume, not score.
- **Reviewers/previews**: map outlets by payment policy before budgeting (labeled previews/runthroughs are paid; reviews are review-copy only). Table: `references/industry-map.md`.
- **Contests**: Cardboard Edison Award (annual, unpublished games, industry judges), Ion Award (SaltCON), BGG community PnP contests — cheap credibility for sell sheets.
- **Post-launch community**: after delivery, the work shifts to BGG forums and rating comments, negative reviews (respond once, never argue), Discord after fulfillment, and a stated fan-content policy. Bridge: `board-game-crowdfunding` owns backer comms until delivery; the living rules FAQ belongs to `board-game-rules-writing`.

## Common pitfalls

- **Idea-guy syndrome** — pitching a concept, not a blind-playtested game. Manifests as "how do I protect my idea?" Publishers won't sign NDAs; execution is the moat.
- **The unsolicited parcel** — mailing a prototype uninvited. Violates stated policy everywhere; the package goes unopened or binned.
- **Signed without reversion** — no fixed end date or POD loophole; the game sits unpublished while rights are tied up for years (Jolly reports losing games for 2–10 years).
- **Royalty-base ambush** — "10% of profits" or an undefined base nets less than 5% of net receipts. Fix the definition before celebrating the rate.
- **Over-arted submission** — commissioning art before signing wastes money and signals inflexibility; publishers redevelop visuals anyway (Stonemaier explicitly says don't).
- **One-publisher monogamy** — submitting sequentially and waiting months per publisher; parallel-submit unless exclusivity is requested.
- **Kickstarter-first thinking** — launching with no audience because "the game is done." Pre-launch funnel comes first → `board-game-crowdfunding`.
- **Review/preview blur** — paying for coverage presented as a review invites community backlash; previews must be labeled and reviews unpaid.
- **Convention spray-and-pray** — pitching every booth instead of the 5–10 publishers with a matching slot; wastes goodwill and marks you as green.
- **Calendar denial** — promising backers/partners a shelf date under 18 months from signing; the timeline eats the promise.
- **Day-job resignation letter** — treating one signing as a career. One licensed mid-tier game typically pays four figures total, spread over years.
