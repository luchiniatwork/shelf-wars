# Campaign Timeline — Deep Reference Checklist

Load this when the user is planning or running a live campaign: pre-launch build-up, launch week, mid-campaign, endgame, or post-campaign operations. Numbers are sourced from Jamey Stegmaier's Kickstarter Lessons (stonemaiergames.com, lessons cited by number), the BoardGameWire Chroma Arcana postmortem (2025), and Gamefound/Kickstarter platform data (2024–25). Folklore figures are marked [contested].

---

## 1. Pre-launch: T−6 months to T−1 month

The campaign is decided here. Stegmaier #230 ("Is Your Crowd Big Enough?") is the gate.

### T−6 to T−3 months — audience infrastructure
- [ ] Landing page live with ONE call to action: email signup. Offer a reason (PnP, rulebook PDF, art newsletter).
- [ ] Email cadence started (monthly value content, not asks). Planning heuristic: warm-list → backer conversion 5–10%+ [contested — agency folklore].
- [ ] Community presence chosen and staffed: BGG thread + images to thumb, Discord server, OR one FB group. Do not open all three and ghost them.
- [ ] BGG entry submitted with box art; track fans and thumbs (Stegmaier's demand metric — Epoch failed at 36 fans / 12 thumbs).
- [ ] Playtesting visible: public PnP or TTS/Screentop build (Chroma Arcana: free Screentop play + Discord games with the creators).
- [ ] Review prototypes ordered and sent to 3–6 video reviewers (2–3 months lead time; the video is forever — prototype must photograph well, #26).
- [ ] Personal outreach list drafted (friends, family, colleagues) for day-one individual emails (#16).
- [ ] Backed 5–10 other projects yourself (#2 — credibility + platform literacy).

### T−3 to T−1 months — assets and math
- [ ] Funding goal computed from the full worksheet (load `pledge-math.md`), including personal-investment line (#7).
- [ ] Tiers priced (MSRP −40% + shipping subsidy, 9-ified; #201) and shipping table quoted by real fulfillment partners per region.
- [ ] Project page drafted inside the platform (anyone can start a draft; preview link auto-forwards at launch, #39).
- [ ] Main video + separate gameplay/how-to video produced (#6, #157); hero GIFs for motion (#162).
- [ ] FAQ answers pre-written in a doc, ready to paste at launch (FAQ can't be created pre-launch, #39).
- [ ] Platform pre-launch page LIVE (months early — Chroma Arcana's follow-up gathered 4,300 followers this way).
- [ ] Rulebook written and linked ("imperative", #39 — a Word/PDF draft is fine).
- [ ] Risks & Challenges written with real risks (mandatory, text-only; specificity signals competence).
- [ ] Kickstarter project submitted for platform review days early (#16); on Gamefound, pre-launch page and FAQ can be fully built ahead (Laakso).

### T−1 month to T−1 week — final push
- [ ] Pre-launch ads/previews ONLY in the final week (#245): 1–2 ads + 1–2 preview videos, each linking to email capture or the pre-launch page; show launch date only if certain.
- [ ] Facebook lead ads running if budget allows ($1–3/lead planning figure [contested]).
- [ ] Cross-promo partners lined up (2–4 similar-scope live or upcoming campaigns, #51).
- [ ] Launch-day logistics: day off work, update templates staged, comment-response plan, Google Analytics/referral tracking enabled.
- [ ] Launch slot chosen: mid-morning (#16), conventionally Tue–Thu, avoiding holidays; campaign ~30 days ending in the same calendar month (#133).

### Launch-readiness gate (#230) — do not launch until you can answer:
- [ ] How many email subscribers, and what did the last 3 emails' open/click rates look like?
- [ ] How many platform pre-launch followers?
- [ ] BGG fans/thumbs trending up?
- [ ] Can you name the ~100 people who will back in the first 24h? (Friends/family/playtesters/community.)
- [ ] If all numbers feel thin: delay. "You don't need to launch today" (#68).

## 2. Launch day and first 72 hours

- [ ] Submit/launch mid-morning; confirm page, video, tiers, shipping table all render correctly.
- [ ] FAQ posted within the first hour (from pre-drafted answers, #39).
- [ ] Personal individual emails to the day-one list — template + personal line each. Stegmaier: 50–70% click-through vs 2–10% mass email (#16). Ask them to *look*, not to back.
- [ ] Newsletter blast to the warm list (they opted in — mass email is fine for them).
- [ ] One social post per platform; one sponsored FB post to page followers (#16). No spam.
- [ ] Thank every early backer personally in comments/DMs; harvest questions into the FAQ.
- [ ] End-of-day grateful update (#16). Then sleep.
- [ ] Days 2–3: keep thanking; watch referral data; note which tier is moving (anchor check).
- [ ] Funding fast? Announce it plainly, thank, pivot messaging to stretch goals and golden goose.
- [ ] Funding slow? Do NOT panic-pivot price/goal/title. Work the #95 sequence: fix page/tiers first, then outreach.

## 3. Mid-campaign (the slump)

The U-curve is structural (#95): launch spike → trough → final-48h spike. Plan for the trough.

- [ ] Update cadence: every 2–4 days; mix content (art, design diaries, playthroughs) with milestones. Backer-only updates for substance (#99).
- [ ] After each update: 30 minutes answering the comments it generates (#90).
- [ ] Micro-goals and social milestones (backer counts, community achievements) to give people a reason to act *now* (#31, #95).
- [ ] Prune tiers with 0 backers; message 1–2-backer tiers and migrate them (#95).
- [ ] Reorder the page: best performing pitch to the top (#39 allows edits during campaign).
- [ ] Personal $1 appeals to your network ("back it for a buck") — $1 backers get updates and upgrade later (#95, #113).
- [ ] Cross-promos go live in both campaigns' updates (#51).
- [ ] Value-first outreach to bloggers/podcasts: offer them content their audience wants, never "write about me" (#95).
- [ ] Ads if the page converts: BGG banners were Stonemaier's best channel; Facebook best scalable channel (Chroma Arcana). Reddit/Google ads ≈ 0 return in the Chroma case.
- [ ] Never beg. "Treat backers like guests at a party you're hosting" (#95).
- [ ] If truly unfundable: cancel, gather feedback, relaunch with a crowd (Epoch did; relaunch is normal, not shameful).

## 4. Endgame (final 72 hours)

- [ ] 60-hour personal reminder to $1 and low tiers — they do NOT get Kickstarter's 48h reminder email (#113).
- [ ] Final-stretch content staged: golden-goose push, "last chance" updates, live stream/playthrough if you have the audience (#210).
- [ ] Cross-promo partners post their final mentions.
- [ ] Expect the surge: final-48h pledges are the norm, not the exception (#95).
- [ ] Final update within hours of close: total raised, what happens next (pledge manager timing), heartfelt thanks.

## 5. Post-campaign

### Weeks 1–4
- [ ] Funds arrive ~2 weeks after close (platform-dependent).
- [ ] Pledge manager opens ~2–4 weeks post-campaign: addresses, shipping fees, taxes, add-on upsell, late pledges. Fee ~5% of campaign + processing [contested — verify current rate card].
- [ ] Late pledges ON (Gamefound native, BackerKit, or KS's native late pledges since May 2024). Gamefound's 2024 aggregate: $71m PM/late-pledge vs $85m live — real money.
- [ ] Update cadence: monthly minimum even when "nothing happened." Silence is the #1 refund driver.

### Production to delivery
- [ ] Manufacturing per `board-game-manufacturing` (proofing ladder: don't skip PPC).
- [ ] Order ~3% extra units (Stegmaier: 150 of 5,300 Euphoria; nearly all consumed by replacements).
- [ ] Minimize SKUs/versions — wrong-version shipments are the top fulfillment error (Euphoria).
- [ ] Freight to regional hubs; VAT prepaid at port for "EU-friendly" delivery (#212); commercial invoices for EU retail backers.
- [ ] EU GPSR (EU 2023/988, in force 13 Dec 2024): EU-established responsible person appointed (EU hub or authorized-representative service), their name + address in the offer and on the product/label, technical documentation file kept, recall procedure defined. Rewards shipped to EU consumers = distance sales (Art. 16).
- [ ] EU EPR packaging registrations done per destination country (Germany: VerpackG/LUCID before shipping; France; Spain) or confirmed in writing as covered by the fulfillment hub; watch the incoming PPWR.
- [ ] Address-update email before shipping charges finalize (#69); automated address updates where possible (#164).
- [ ] Shipping-season peace (#130): publish the hub-by-hub schedule, answer tracking questions fast, have replacement parts ready.
- [ ] Timeline slips: announce early, once, with a new date — then hit it.
- [ ] Refunds: honor pre-fulfillment where possible; Stegmaier's money-back-guarantee experiment (#168) found requests rare when trust is high.
- [ ] After delivery: replacement-parts policy is forever. Your next campaign's first 48h is built here.
